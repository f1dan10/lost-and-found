# Application pitch

## Name

**Lost&Found**

## Motivation

MIT already has places to hand in a lost item, but the person who finds something and the person who lost it have no single place where they can find each other.

## Key features

Imagine you are walking out of Stata and notice a water bottle on a bench. You want to help, but you are not going to carry it to MIT Police on the other side of campus. With **Quick Report**, you do not have to. You take one photo, pick the building from a list, and keep walking. The bottle stays where it is. This matters because a finder's willingness to help is real but small, so reporting has to cost less than walking past. The owner gains a report that would otherwise never have existed.

Now imagine you are the one who lost the bottle. You were in three buildings today and you do not know which one it is in. Instead of searching group chats, dormspam, and several desks, you open the **Two-Sided Map** and see what has been found in those buildings. If your bottle is not there yet, you post a lost report, mark the buildings where it might be, and get notified when a matching item is found in one of them. Finders and owners end up looking at the same map, which is the whole point.

The last feature is **Named Claims**. Only people with an MIT account can use Lost&Found, and when you click "This is mine", the claim is recorded under your account and shown to the finder. This does not make a false claim impossible, but a false claimant is then a named community member rather than an anonymous account, so a finder can post a clear photo without worrying about who replies.
