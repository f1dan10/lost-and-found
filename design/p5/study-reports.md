# P5 study reports

## Participant 1

Participant 1 signed up as a new user. They worked through the lost report, the match, the claim, pickup and thanks, the map and the filters, with one explanation from me, on the map.

They found the Report button at once and praised the category icons as "friendly and approachable". They understood the spot field unprompted ("where you were last with that item"), chose three buildings without trouble, and wondered aloud whether outdoor places could be reported, a gap we had not considered. They spotted the "may be yours" notice straight away, judged that the found bracelet was theirs, claimed it, marked it picked up and wrote a thank-you note with no hesitation. The main flow works.

The one problem came on the map. Searching "Media Lab" also showed markers on other buildings, and only one item was actually in the Media Lab; the others were in the Muckley Building and the Morris & Sophie Chang Building. They were confused until I explained that a lost report can list several buildings, so the same item appears under each one it might be in. Afterwards they said the search and the dots on the map were convenient.

In the filter task they found sort and category chips, correctly guessed that the two buttons switch between list and grid, and preferred the grid view. They liked that one search box covers buildings, items and people.

Overall: strong on the core flow, weak on the multi-building model, which shows through the map.

## Participant 2

Participant 2 completed the tasks but showed more friction. These notes come from my summary, as the transcript was lost.

They found the plus button easily and liked the category section. Before signing in they asked what happens if they forget their password; the app has no recovery. On the report form they could not tell which fields were required and which optional, and did not realize they could type into the buildings box or add several buildings. They did understand the spot field.

They struggled to notice the notification after posting; a pop-up would have helped. They also wanted to understand how matches were found. It mattered to them that no AI was involved, only simple checks of category and building, and they suggested AI could improve matching. They did not understand the Returned section.

They liked filtering by location and category, the search across all report fields, the multiple views and the map. They proposed private groups, such as one per dorm, so outsiders do not see reports that are only relevant locally.

Overall: the browsing tools landed well, and the participant saw value in the app, but the buildings box, the notice and the Returned section need work before a newcomer can use them unaided. Their wish to understand the matching suggests that a short plain explanation of how a match is made would build trust.
