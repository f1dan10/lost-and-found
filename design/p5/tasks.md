# P5 user study tasks

Deployed, populated app: https://lostandfound.mit-sdg.dev/. About 40 minutes of tasks, 20 minutes of debrief. The participant gets a scenario card on paper, naming one item they lost (already reported as found in the app by another user, in a shared building) and one item they find. Rows are in the order they are run: Task 1 creates the lost report that Task 2 relies on, Task 2 creates the claim for Task 3, and Task 4 creates the found report for Task 7.

| # | Title | Instruction given to the participant | Rationale: what we hope to learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Post a lost item | "You've just heard about Lost&Found, a lost-and-found website for MIT. Create an account, then use it to get help finding the item on your card." | Covers sign-up, the report form and the multi-building picker, which is the least familiar control. A walkthrough shows the controls exist. A user shows whether they find the report button, understand that several buildings can be chosen, tell optional fields from required ones, and know the report was posted. |
| 2 | Find out if it has turned up | "A few hours later, you want to know whether anyone has handed in something that could be yours. If so, get it back." | Tests whether the notification and "Waiting on you" are noticed without a prompt, whether the participant picks the right item among a match and a decoy, and whether "This is mine" feels like a commitment. Afterwards: do they notice their lost report closed itself? |
| 3 | Collect it and say thanks | "You have walked to the place and have the item in your hands. Record that, and let the finder know you are grateful." | Tests the end of the main flow: finding the pickup place, "I picked it up" and the thank-you note. Evaluation: does the participant know where their claimed item now is, and that the finder was told? |
| 4 | Report a found item | "On your way out you find the item on your card. You are in a hurry and want its owner to get it without you waiting around. Report it." | Tests the finder flow and the photo upload. We learn whether "leave it where it is" is understood and whether the found/lost choice is clear. |
| 5 | Use the map | "A friend is going to the Media Lab. Using the map, tell me what has been found there." (3 items) | Tests whether the map is found, whether markers are understood as buildings, and whether lost and found are told apart. Having a known answer shows whether the information was read correctly. |
| 6 | Search and filter | "A friend thinks they left a black umbrella in Building 7. Find out if anyone found it. Then tell me how many electronics are currently found, and what has been returned to owners recently." | Tests search (words and place), category filters, list and grid view and the Returned section in one multi-step task. Two umbrellas act as a decoy. We learn which tool people reach for first and whether Found, Lost and Returned are distinguishable by name. |
| 7 | Check on your found item | "Later, you want to know whether the item you reported has been picked up." (The facilitator claims it from a second account during Task 6.) | Tests evaluation on the finder's side: can they find the status and understand it unaided? Also whether not seeing who claimed it surprises them. |
